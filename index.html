OpenImpact: see where your support goes

A giving platform where communities raise needs, verified experts vet the proposed solutions and funders release money only as milestones are met. Anyone can see where the money went and what it achieved.

Who it helps
Communities with unmet needs, experts whose vetting should count and funders who want proof their money worked
How we'd measure it
Needs that reach funding, time from proposal to funding and the share of funds released against verified milestones
Business model
Funders pay a small fee on money released through verified milestones.
My role
Came up with the idea and designed the business model

Ecotein: fish-feed protein made from plastic waste

Only a small fraction of the plastic ever made has been recycled, and fish farms still depend on fishmeal from wild-caught fish. Ecotein uses microbes to turn waste PET into a protein ingredient for fish and shrimp feed. It's based in Singapore and sells to feed mills across Southeast Asia.

Our financial model showed that breaking down the plastic costs more than fishmeal sells for. The open question: a cheaper process or a higher-value market?

Customer
Fish and shrimp feed mills in Vietnam, Indonesia and Thailand, starting with Singapore fish farms
Competition
Fishmeal, plus single-cell proteins such as Calysta and Unibio
What we built
Competitive map, market sizing, value proposition, product spec, financial model and funding plan
My role
Did the research and built the financial model

About

I've been a software engineer since 2014, building the systems that move and make sense of data at scale, along with the tools other engineers rely on every day. I came to software for its stability and stayed because I found I love building things.

Over the years I've learned that the technology is rarely the hard part. What matters is whether people can trust what you've built and whether it truly makes their lives better.

What I care about most is using what I have to help others, whether that's code, time or the kind of organizing that brings people together. I came to USC's Iovine and Young Academy to add business and design to my engineering, so I can shape the whole product and not just the systems underneath it.
